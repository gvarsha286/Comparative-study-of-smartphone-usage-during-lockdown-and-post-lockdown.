📱 Comparative Study of Smartphone Usage During Lockdown and Post-Lockdown

This project explores how smartphone usage patterns changed from the lockdown period to the post-lockdown phase. Using survey responses collected from students across different academic backgrounds, the study analyzes behavioural shifts, screen-time trends, and user perceptions toward smartphone dependency.

🧪 Methodology
1. Data Collection

A structured questionnaire was shared via WhatsApp, email, and other digital channels.

Participants included students from various academic streams.

Responses were cleaned, coded, and organized in Microsoft Excel for analysis.

2. Tools Used

Microsoft Excel (data cleaning, coding, and statistical testing)

3. Statistical Methods

Paired T-Test (Two Sample Means):
Assessed whether the difference between smartphone usage during and after lockdown was statistically significant.

T-Test Assuming Equal Variances:
Evaluated whether variations between groups were meaningful or occurred randomly.

Chi-Square Test:
Tested the relationship between age and frequency of internet usage.

📊 Key Findings

Smartphone usage showed a significant decrease after the lockdown ended.

The average number of hours spent on phones was higher during lockdown.

There is no significant association between age and frequency of internet usage.

Most participants agreed that excessive smartphone use is unhealthy or unnecessary.

✔️ Conclusion

The study highlights clear behavioural changes as routines normalized after lockdown. Reduced screen time, stable internet usage patterns across age groups, and stronger awareness about digital overuse collectively reflect evolving user habits in the post-lockdown environment.
